## A. Work on this policy

1. **Elazar, Y. and Antoniak, M. "LLM-Generated or Human-Written? Comparing Review and Non-Review Papers
   on ArXiv."** arXiv:2601.17036 (v1 19 Jan 2026, v2 9 Sep 2026), cs.DL. Comment field: "Accepted to
   Findings of EMNLP 2026". https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17036 . Code: https://github.com/yanaiela/llm-review-arxiv
   - Opened: **full text** (PDF, 19 pages; text saved in `data/priorwork/pdf/`).
   - What it does: tests arXiv's stated reason. Classifies review versus non-review papers with an LLM
     classifier ("92.0% F1" on their annotations) and estimates LLM-generated share with two detectors.
   - Data: the Kaggle arXiv snapshot "sampled on December 15th, 2025"; papers "published between 2020 and
     2025"; up to 500 papers per month per domain (CS, mathematics, statistics, physics) and per CS subcategory.
   - Findings quoted: review papers "21.4%" versus non-review "14.0%" LLM-generated (adjusted, CS); "almost
     six times more non-review papers generated by LLMs than review papers"; "Computers & Society papers
     could be censored by 50%, while Computer Vision will only lose 3% of its papers."
   - What it does **not** do: it does not measure what happened after the rule. Its figures on impact are
     projections from the pre-policy share of review papers. It has no before/after series, no displacement
     test and no retitling test.
   - Limitation they state, which this project shares: data are "restricted to papers posted to arXiv rather
     than submitted to arXiv. This data is not publicly available, and our attempts to request this data
     were not successful."
   - **This is the closest paper and must be cited and distinguished.** Their classifier and annotations
     are a possible second measurement for a robustness check (their code is public).

2. **Lin, J., Shan, R., Zhu, J., Xi, Y., Yu, Y., Zhang, W. "Stop DDoS Attacking the Research Community with
   AI-Generated Survey Papers."** arXiv:2510.09686 (9 Oct 2025), cs.CY. Comment field: "Accepted by NeurIPS
   2025 (Position Track)". https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.09686
   - Opened: abstract page, and the HTML full text through a summarising fetch (so numbers below are as
     reported by that fetch; check against the paper before quoting).
   - What it does: position paper, three weeks before the rule. Trend analysis of CS arXiv papers 2020-2024
     whose titles contain "survey", "review", "overview" or "taxonomy" ("a total of 10,063 papers"), AI-text
     detector scores, and counts of authors posting more than 3 surveys in a month.
   - Relevance: the best published description of the "flood" before the rule, and precedent for a
     title-keyword definition of a survey. It suggests moderators flag prolific survey authors; it does not
     study the rule.

## B. LLM-written or LLM-assisted papers

5. **Liang, W. et al. "Mapping the Increasing Use of LLMs in Scientific Papers."** arXiv:2404.01268 (1 Apr 2024).
   Opened: abstract page. 950,965 papers from arXiv, bioRxiv and Nature-portfolio journals, Jan 2020-Feb 2024;
   fastest growth in computer science ("up to 17.5%"). Journal version: Liang, Zhang, Wu et al., "Quantifying
   large language model usage in scientific papers", Nature Human Behaviour 2025, doi:10.1038/s41562-025-02273-8
   (metadata only, Crossref).
6. **Kobak, D., Gonzalez-Marquez, R., Horvat, E.-A., Lause, J. "Delving into LLM-assisted writing in biomedical
   publications through excess vocabulary."** Science Advances 11(27), 2025, doi:10.1126/sciadv.adt3813;
   arXiv:2406.07016. Opened: abstract page. Over 15 million PubMed abstracts 2010-2024; "at least 13.5%" of 2024
   abstracts LLM-processed. Useful method idea: track excess words rather than classify single papers. It
   could become an add-on analysis (did "delve"-style vocabulary in CS surveys change after the rule?).
7. **Kusumegi, K., Yang, X., Ginsparg, P. et al. "Scientific production in the era of large language models."**
   Science, 2025, doi:10.1126/science.adw3000. Metadata only (Crossref). Reported to cover preprint servers
   including arXiv; one author is arXiv's founder. Open it before citing any finding.
8. Further candidates, metadata only (Crossref): Paklina, Parshakov, Rapoport, "LLM-assisted writing and
   citation advantage...", Scientometrics 2026, doi:10.1007/s11192-026-05805-9; Nazarovets, "The Marker Drift
   Problem: Measuring LLM-Assisted Writing with Unstable Lexical Indicators", 2026 preprint,
   doi:10.31222/osf.io/xs64q_v1 (relevant warning: word markers drift, as survey wording may after the rule).
9. Automatic survey writing (shows why surveys are cheap to produce): Wang, Guo, Yao et al., "AutoSurvey: Large
   Language Models Can Automatically Write Surveys", NeurIPS 2024, arXiv:2406.10252, doi:10.52202/079017-3655
   (metadata only; a web-search summary says it charts survey counts on arXiv to April 2024). "SurveyX:

## Novelty verdict (precise)
- **Found:** one peer-reviewed paper about this rule (Elazar and Antoniak, Findings of EMNLP 2026). It
  evaluates the rule's *justification* and *projects* who would be affected, using papers posted up to 2025.
- **Not found:** any study that measures the *observed* change in survey and position papers on arXiv after
  31 Oct 2025, any displacement analysis across arXiv categories, any retitling analysis, or any check of
  journal-ref/DOI compliance.
- **So the specific contribution is open as far as this search can tell:** a before/after (interrupted time
  series) measurement with comparison fields, displacement tests, a wording-change test and a compliance
  check, using a hand-validated classifier.
- **Confidence: moderate, not high.** Reasons: (a) two of three bibliographic databases were unavailable
  today; (b) the rule is 11 months old and an obvious target, so a competing analysis could be on a blog,
  in a workshop, or under review; (c) Elazar and Antoniak have code and data that could be extended to
  2026 in a revision. Their v2 (Sep 2026) still has no post-policy measurement.
- **What would reduce the risk:** rerun the cached searches when OpenAlex and Semantic Scholar answer; search
  arXiv listing cs.DL and cs.CY for 2026 by hand; post the pre-registration (`03-design.md`) with a date.
